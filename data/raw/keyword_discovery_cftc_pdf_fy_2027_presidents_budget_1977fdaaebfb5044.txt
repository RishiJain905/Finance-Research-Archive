The CFTC is requesting a budget of $410.0 million and 650 full-time equivalents (FTE) to operate the agency effectively and support our mission in FY 2027. The FY 2027 request is an increase of $45 million, or 12.3 percent above the FY 2026 enacted budget. This proposed budget reflects the level of resources needed for the CFTC to maintain and enhance its role as the primary regulator of the U.S. futures, swaps, and options markets, especially given the rapid innovation in our markets. The CFTC must keep up with the pace of innovation and modernize its technological capabilities in order to effectively carry out its mission. The CFTC also must be appropriately equipped to effectively regulate the novel products and platforms emerging in our financial markets. The adoption of blockchain technologies, crypto assets, and smart contracts is introducing new methods for trading, clearing, settling, and collateralizing commodity price exposure. Artificial intelligence and autonomous systems are streamlining processes and evolving systems that execute orders at speeds and volumes far beyond human capacity. And a wide range of new retail-focused derivatives products have exploded in popularity, requiring the agency to develop new rules to protect retail investors in what has historically been an institutionally focused market. The CFTC must invest in its own technology and capabilities in order to effectively oversee these new innovations and ensure that they flourish in the U.S. The CFTC also must focus on its core markets, including agriculture and energy to ensure that these markets continue to work for our farmers, ranchers, energy producers, and small businesses. These markets are vital to participants to hedge production costs and delivery prices so that American consumers can enjoy stable prices. We will also seek to strengthen the resilience and integrity of our markets, while continuing to invest resources to ensure our derivatives markets operate free from fraud, manipulation, and other trading abuses. This proposed budget would help the CFfC fulfill its mission as the U.S. derivatives markets continue to rapidly evolve. This pursuit furthers the agency’s broader mission to promote the integrity, resilience, and vibrancy of the U.S. derivatives markets through sound regulation. The CFTC uses its enforcement authority to, among other things: 1) shut down fraudulent schemes and seek to immediately preserve customer assets through asset freezes and receivership orders; 2) uncover and stop manipulative and disruptive trading; 3) ensure that markets, firms, and participants subject to the C FTC’s oversight meet their obligations, including their financial integrity and reporting obligations, as applicable; 4) ban certain defendants from trading in its markets and bar them from being registered; and 5) obtain orders requiring defendants to pay restitution, disgorgement, and civil monetary penalties. Potential violations include fraud, misappropriation, price manipulation, use of a manipulative or deceptive device, disruptive trading practices, and other abuses concerning commodity derivatives, swaps, and commodity transactions that threaten market integrity, market participants, or the general public. The CFTC’s whistleblower awards are equal, in the aggregate amount to at least 10 percent but not more than 30 percent of the monetary sanctions actually collected in the Commission’s action or a related action. Section 748 of the Dodd-Frank Act also established the CFTC Customer Protection Fund (CPF) for the payment of awards to whistleblowers, through the Whistleblower Program, and the funding of customer education initiatives designed to help customers protect themselves against fraud or other violations of the CEA or the rules or regulations thereunder. The CFTC undertakes and maintains customer education initiatives through the Customer Education Program. Management of the Whistleblower Office The Whistleblower Office (WBO) has three essential functions: • Process Whistleblower Submissions. The WBO receives, tracks, and handles whistleblower submissions and inquiries , often communicating with whistleblowers or their counsel to address questions or concerns. • Coordinate with C FTC Divisions and Outside Agencies. The WBO answers questions from CFTC staff and others regarding the whistleblower program and guides the handling of whistleblower matters as needed during examination, investigation, and litigation. The WBO also approves referrals of whistleblower-identifying information to outside agencies. • Administer Claims Process. The WBO receives and tracks whistleblower award claims, gathers and prepares the adjudicatory records for the Whistleblower Claims Review Staff (CRS), advises the CRS as needed on the whistleblower provisions and rules, and memorializes the CRS’s decisions. Those determinations lead to Final Orders of the Commission through a review process that the WBO also coordinates. Finally, the WBO processes award claims that do not meet minimum requirements under the Whistleblower Rules, 17 CFR part 165. In FY 202 5, the CFTC issued tw0 (2) orders for whistleblower awards, granting three applications totaling approximately $4.6 million, to individuals who voluntarily provided original information that led to successful enforcement actions. All whistleblower award payments are made from the CPF established by Congress, financed entirely through monetary sanctions paid to the CFTC by violators of the CEA. In FY 2026 and FY 2027, the WBO will continue its communications with market participants and voluntary whistleblowers about the protections and incentives afforded by the Whistleblower Program. The WBO will also continue processing whistleblower submissions and award claims wi th the goal of improving efficiency. To do this, OCEO provides engaging fraud awareness educational content for CFTC.gov, exhibits at conferences and trade shows, and works with other authorities and private organizations to amplify the reach of its anti -fraud message. In FY 2025, OCEO focused on three priorities: • Customer Outreach: Increase awareness of fraud, scams, and other violations through strategic outreach campaigns that target affected market customers or communities that could influence customers, such as podcasters. • Customer Education: Warn customers of potential CEA violations through advisories, outreach, social media, and email. • Partnerships: Build and utilize partnerships with F ederal, state, and nonprofit organizations to amplify OCEO’s anti-fraud messaging and identify new scam trends. Operation of the CFTC Customer Protection Fund The CPF is a revolving fund established under section 748 of the Dodd- Frank Act. The C FTC shall deposit civil monetary penalties, disgorgements, and interest it collects in covered administrative or judicial enforcement actions into the CPF whenever the balance in the CPF at the time of the deposit is less than or equal to $100 million. The C FTC pays whistleblower awards and finances customer education initiatives from the CPF but does not deposit restitution awarded to victims into the CPF.
